Free Career Support

You never have to search alone

These peer groups, ministries, nonprofits, and government programs cost nothing and give you accountability, coaching, and people in your corner.

Start here: join a free peer Job Search Council at Never Search Alone, and a weekly group at the Career Ministry Network. Both are 100% free, open to everyone, and built so you never search alone.
Volunteer & Internships

Build experience while you search

Volunteering keeps your skills sharp, expands your network, and closes resume gaps — and studies show it leads to interviews faster. Internships and micro-internships open doors for students, recent grads, and career changers alike.

Leadership Programs

Launch straight into leadership

Leadership Development Programs (LDPs) are paid, structured rotational tracks that fast-track new grads and career changers into management. They are highly competitive — apply early, since most open applications in the fall. All are free to apply (a program that charges a fee is a red flag).

Executive Search & Staffing Agencies

Recruiters who work for you, free

Staffing agencies and search firms are paid by employers, not job seekers — never pay one a fee. Register with the firms that specialize in your field, and get on the radar of retained executive search firms at your level.

About

Built for job seekers

Career Compass is a free, carefully organized directory of 800+ vetted job boards, AI tools, and career communities. Nothing here is sponsored — every resource earns its place. A few tips for using it:

1Pick 2–3 boards, not 20

One general board (Indeed or LinkedIn) plus the niche board for your function beats spraying applications everywhere.

2Filter by your function

Use the function chips at the top — Finance, HR, Tech, Creative and more — to instantly see only what applies to you.

3Select your state

Choosing a state surfaces its official job bank, federal jobs near you, and free in-person career centers.

4Tailor with AI, edit by hand

Jobscan and Teal (AI Tools tab) show missing keywords — but only you know your real story.

5Join one support group this week

Accountability groups measurably shorten searches. The Free Career Support tab has options for everyone.

6Use your library card

Most public libraries give free access to LinkedIn Learning and career coaching — see the support tab.

The Navigator

Meet Bradley Matican

Career Compass was built — and is maintained — by Bradley Matican, a mediator, HR professional, and organizational-behavior specialist based in Fort Lee, New Jersey. His work has always lived at the intersection of people and work: resolving disputes, building talent and culture strategies, and studying how people and organizations actually behave.

Career Compass grew out of a simple observation: the best job-search resources are scattered, paywalled, or buried in lists of fifty links — while the people who need them most are out of work and short on money and time. So every resource here is free for job seekers, organized the way a career coach would organize it, and vetted by hand. Know a resource that belongs here? Use the Suggest a Resource tab — it reaches Bradley directly.
